  1. Part I · The Harbour Years · 1948–1963
  2. 1948Workshop

    The First Bench

    Einar Voss rents a single room above the harbour chandlery and spends the polar night repairing ship compasses by lamplight. The bench he builds that first winter is still in use.

  3. 1954Product

    The Voss Chronometer

    Seven prototypes, one keeper. Nordlys ships its first in-house marine chronometer to the Hurtigruten fleet — forty-four seconds of drift across the whole Atlantic crossing.

  4. 1963Method

    Circle of Light

    The workshop moves into a purpose-built observatory on the headland. Sextant mirrors are ground in-house for the first time, under a glass roof that never quite stops humming.

  5. Part II · Open Water · 1971–1988
  6. 1971Trial

    The Storm Test

    A February gale takes the test mast clean off the headland. The Mk III theodolite bolted to it is recovered from the scree — still reading true to within a minute of arc.

  7. 1979Succession

    Second Generation

    Astrid Voss takes the keys and immediately retools the line in titanium. Half the old guard resigns; the other half stays long enough to be proven wrong.

  8. 1988Export

    Meridian Line

    First export licences signed in Tokyo. Instruments leave Tromsø in cedar crates stamped with the aurora mark, bound for survey crews on three continents.

  9. Part III · The Keepers · 1996–2024
  10. 1996Decision

    The Quartz Question

    The board votes 4–3 against going fully quartz. Nordlys keeps its hybrid movement — mechanical heart, electronic conscience — and bets the whole ledger on it.

  11. 2005Fire

    The Archive Fire

    Fourteen minutes from alarm to roof gone. The instruments survive the vault; the paper archive mostly doesn't. The workshop reopens in a year and two months.

  12. 2014Reopening

    The Keepers' School

    The rebuilt east wing opens as a museum and apprenticeship school. Twelve students a year, one rule: nothing leaves the bench until it would survive the harbour.

  13. 2024Today

    Seventy-Six Years

    Same bench, same lamp, new hands. Apprentices now arrive from twelve countries to learn the old tolerances — and the spine of this archive stays unwritten below.
